Horse Rules

A word of caution: Horseback combat can be a bit complicated, so make sure you are familiar with the base game mechanics first, especially concerning view cones.

Horse Stats

A horse has numerous stats that influence its performance. Many of these are shared with normal characters and can be viewed at Character Sheet & Stats. The stats unique to horses/work differently for horses are listed below:

Initiative

A horse's initiative acts as a bonus/malus to their rider's initiative.

Temperament

Influences how likely a horse is to follow orders.

Carry Capacity

How much can a horse carry directly.

Charge Acceleration

How hard a horse can hit during charge attacks.

Charge Manoeuvrability

How much a horse can change its course when charging.

Size

A horse takes up 2m by 2m space. A rider is considered to be on top of the horse. Any attack that can reach the horse can also reach the rider.

View Cones

A horse has a default view cone of 90 degrees. A rider shares the same view cone as the horse.

Normally your view cone can be rotated freely at any time, so long as it fits the current view cone restrictions. On horseback, you cannot rotate your view cone freely. Horses need 1m movement speed to rotate their view cone by 45 degrees, and once your turn ends your view cone is stuck in that position until your next turn. This act of rotating your view cone does not trigger opportunity attacks.

On horseback, you still inflict but are not affected by view cone restrictions.

Movement

Rotating your view cone by 45 degrees takes 1m of movement speed. You can only move directly forwards or backwards with reference to your view cone.

Moving backwards costs twice the movement speed. Horses can only jump forwards.

Riders can mount/dismount with a regular action + all remaining movement speed.

Horses can unlock new forms of movement during training.

Attacking a rider

When attacking a rider, you can choose to target either rider or horse. Targeting the horse will use the horse's stats and defenses for calculations.

Movement attacks

A weapon move that successfully moves the rider will knock them off, causing them to fall prone. Where they fall will depend on terrain, but usually they will fall in the direction they were moved.
Horses can be moved.

Grappling attacks

A successful grappling attempt against the rider will result in the rider being dragged off the horse. The rider will still be standing, but be locked in a grapple with the attacker. Where they dismount depends on the attacker and available space.
Horses cannot be grappled.

Opportunity and Zoning attacks

Opportunity or zoning attacks that hit the horse/rider do not remove their movement speed.

Attacking from horseback

When attacking as a rider, you can use most of the weapon moves as normal, however weapon moves that also move you cannot be used normally.

Horses cannot act independently of their rider beyond some simple circumstances. A horse's actions will use the rider's action resources.

Charging on horseback

You may choose to charge in one of 8 directions on a square grid (+ and x) when riding a horse.

  • Charging will use up all your remaining movement speed and and allow the horse to move twice that movement.
  • You must move the entire distance when charging, you cannot stop early.
  • Picture the horse's charge distance split into two equal halves, called the "build up" and the "slow down".

The Build Up

The build up is the first half of the charge movement, when Charge Force builds up. Imagine this as the horse gaining acceleration, reaching top speed at the end of the build up phase.

The Slow Down

The slow down is the latter half of the charge movement, when Charge Force depletes. Imagine this as the horse slowing down to a safe speed after charging.

Charge Force

Every 3m travelled during The Build Up, you add your horse's charge acceleration to the total charge force.
Every 3m travelled during The Slow Down, you remove your horse's charge acceleration from the total charge force
These intervals are 3m travelled on the map, not 3m movement speed expended by the horse.

When charging, there are two forms of damage you can do:

Horse Charge-through

If there are enemies in your charge path, you will run them over with the following rules:

  • Your horse will be considered to have impacted the enemy when they are 1m away. (i.e. adjacent to the horse)
  • DC for the run over status is determined by DC 10 + charge force built up at the moment of impact.
  • Your horse deals its built up charge force as blunt force trauma to run over enemies.
  • If an enemy succeeds their save against the run over check, they will stop the rest of your charge at the point of impact, reflecting the run over blunt force trauma onto the horse.
  • If an enemy fails their save instead, you will pass through them and carry on the rest of the charge.

Weapon Charge

You can use any melee weapon move on horseback and it will grant you the horse's built up charge force as damage, hit chance and DC if the move has a status.

  • The target must still fit in your view cone during the charge.
  • The exception is the on foot charge action, which cannot be done.
  • Ranged attacks do not gain this bonus.

Charge Vulnerability

When charging, any enemy opportunity or zoning attacks against you or your horse will gain your Weapon Charge bonuses.

Charge Manoeuvrability

Each point of charge manoeuvrability allows you to make a 45 degree turn at any point in your charge.
At the start of your charge, you can trade half of your charge acceleration for +1 charge manoeuvrability, up to twice.

Actions

You can use actions at any point in the charge. Charging can be used as a mobility tool, not just for damage.

Temperament Checks

The higher a horse's temperament, the more likely it is to obey your commands.

A temperament check is rolled as such:

  • Roll a d20 and add your horse's temperance + your riding skill against the DC of the check.

Horses roll their temperament checks during the following situations:

  • Having a charge stopped prematurely:
    • Happens when stopped by terrain or by enemies.
    • DC 10 + charge force built up by the horse.
  • Rider being dismounted:
    • DC 20.
  • Horse is under half health:
    • Temperament check is triggered at the start of the turn.
    • DC 10.

Training

Horses have a tiered system of skills. You unlock skills one at a time. Each tier of skills requires a certain number of skills from the tier below to be unlocked before they can be trained.

Horses can be trained during rests by spending time units. How much time a skill requires differs from skill to skill and is listed on the skill. This time is also reduced by the level of your riding skill. (i.e. A trainer with 3 riding skill will take 3 less time units when training a skill.)

Rider Weight Limit

A horse's carrying capacity indicates how heavy the rider can be when riding. This is calculated by the sum of:

  • The rider's equipment load and backpack load.
    • During combat, you can eject your backpack to remove your backpack load from the calculations, just like on foot.
  • The rider's race's weight.
  • The horse's equipment.

Going overweight will cut the horse's movement speed in half and remove their access to their skills.

Going over twice the weight limit will result in the horse being unable to move.

Cart Capacity

When pulling a cart, a horse will multiply it's carrying capacity by the cart's modifier. Horses cannot be ridden into battle when pulling a cart. Multiple horses can pull the same cart, adding their carrying capacities together.

Carts do not count towards a horse's equipment weight.

Feeding and Upkeep

Horses need food every long rest like regular characters and can suffer the same penalties if they don't eat enough.

Horses can eat raw grain or rice and gain the full nutrition. Horses cannot eat meat or fish.

If the environment allows, horses can simply graze instead of needing to be fed.
