Weapon Smithing

General Overview and Rules

Weapon smithing involves going to the weapon's individual wiki page and finding out what parts make up a weapon. Then, expend time-units during short and long rests to craft the individual parts until you have a complete weapon. Detailed below are all the different weapon parts, and how they can be customised. Customisation works off the weapon's/weapon part's base stats, which is what is listed on the individual weapon sheets.

Requirements

To do anything listed below, you require the smithing(weapon) skill, alongside a set of smithing tools. Some customisations may require a pixie with certain masteries alongside the tools and skills, or a set of pixie smithing tools alongside the skill.

Weapon part statistics

Each weapon part has 4 parameters to consider:

  • Material: What the part is made of. There are 3 types: Metal, Wood and Leather.
  • Craft Time: How long it takes to make in time-units.
  • Cost: Value of the part.
  • Weight: Weight of the part.
    The base stats for each weapon part and weapon are the values set when using the standard material types, which are steel bars, medium grade wood and medium grade leather.

Weapon part customisation

Each weapon part has a list of customisation types that can be applied to it. For each customisation type, you may only choose 1 option. Each customisation title has a number next to it, this is the level of weapon smithing skill you need to have to apply that customisation.

Customisation stat interactions

  • Weapons with a negative damage modifier from will always do at least 1 damage.
  • Weapons that deal blunt force trauma with a negative blunt force trauma modifier will always do at least 1 blunt force trauma.
  • Weapons with a negative penetration modifier will not increase enemy effective armour and will be treated as having 0 penetration.
  • Weapons with a positive penetration modifier can bypass the proficiency cap if present.
  • Customisation effects are additive: Having two +50% modifiers will result in a +100% modifier.
  • Round any decimals from calculations up to the next whole number, apart from weight calculations, which are rounded to the next 0.25kg.
  • Some stat modifiers are changed depending on if the weapon is 2-handed. In this case, weapons that are 1-handed/2-handed will receive the appropriate stat modifier depending on if they are used as 1-handed or 2-handed weapons.
  • Status effect DC uses the same calculations as normal saving throws the weapon uses.

Weapon part cost calculations

Each weapon part's final cost is derived as such:

(Total Cost of Material) x (Time-units taken x 0.20) x (Customisation Modifier)

Changes to material or time cost will affect the final price but are not directly stated below in the customisation pros and cons, so be sure to factor those in! Or you can ignore calculating cost if you're never planning on selling your weapon I guess.

Weapon Disassembly

Weapons can be disassembled to their base parts, allowing you to swap individual parts out without the need to reforge the entire weapon.

  • It takes 1 time-unit to remove or attach 1 weapon part from a weapon.
  • Weapon parts are not interchangeable between weapons. Players can ask the GM for exceptions to this rule if two weapons share a part with the same base ingredient costs, craft times, and make sense logically to be interchangeable. (e.g. The long-haft part of a spear and halberd, or the grip part for most one-handed/two-handed weapons)

Weapon Part Smelting/Scrapping

You can scrap individual weapon parts to recover 50% of the materials used to create them.
The time-unit cost and skill requirement to scrap a weapon part is equal to the tier of the material the part is made of. (e.g. A part made of tier 1 materials needs skill 1 in weapon smithing and 1 time-unit to scrap.)

Weapon Part Repair

A broken/damaged weapon part can be repaired with half of the required materials and time-unit cost.

Weapon Parts Customisation List

Melee Weapons

Part: Blade

Customisation - Material:

  • Steel (Skill 2)
    • Standard material, no effects.
  • Iron (Skill 1)
    • -1 damage. (Double for 2-handed weapons)
    • -1 penetration.
    • -50% weapon part time-unit cost.
  • Pixie-forged Steel (Skill 3)
    • +1 damage. (Double for 2-handed weapons)
    • +50% weapon part time-unit cost.
  • Nebulan Starshard (Skill 3)
    • +2 penetration.
    • +50% weapon part time-unit cost.
  • Composite Edge (Skill 3)
    • Needs a pixie with metal mastery or pixie smithing tools.
    • Requires an equal split of Pixie-forged Steel and Nebulan Starshard.
      • If the material cost is 1, then require 1 of each material.
      • If the material cost is odd, then split the cost with 1 more Pixie-forged Steel.
    • +1 damage. (Doubled for 2-handed weapons)
    • +2 penetration.
    • +100% weapon part time-unit cost.
  • Glass Razor (Skill 3)
    • Needs a pixie with metal mastery or pixie smithing tools.
    • Requires Nebulan Starshard.
    • +2 damage. (Doubled for 2-handed weapons)
    • +4 penetration
    • +50% weapon part time-unit cost.
    • Every time the weapon is used, this part breaks has a 50% chance to break. You are refunded half the materials used to craft it.
