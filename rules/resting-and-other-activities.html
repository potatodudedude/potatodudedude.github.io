Resting and Other Activities

Resting is divided into two types, short and long.

Time-units

During each type of rest, you have a resource called time-units to spend. This mechanic represents the amount of time you have to do things like crafting.

If your personal time-units are not enough to complete an action, you can borrow a party member's to finish the job.

  • For actions that have skill and/or tool prerequisites, your party members' time will only count for half as much if they don't also fulfil the prerequisite. There are no "half" time-units, so in such a case a party member must commit their time-units in multiples of 2.
  • This only applies if you spend at least 1 time-unit yourself.

Short Rest

Spend at least 1 hour resting, during which all party members:

  • Heal 1d6 health, with the party's highest medical skill added as a bonus.
    Short rests are limited to once per long rest.

Crafting

You are given 2 time-units during short rests.
With the appropriate tools, you can follow the given recipes to make provisions, craft throwables, medicine, potions etc.

Long Rest

Spend at least 8 hours resting. Long rests are limited to once per day. If you have not short rested for the day, add on the health and time-unit bonuses you would have gotten.
Long resting is required everyday. Failing to do so results in +1 exhaustion for each day that goes by without long resting.

Healing

Each party member may consume food, and the effects depend on how much is eaten:

  • Consume 2 units: Heal for a value equal to half your max health, -1 exhaustion.
    • Extra bonuses may apply depending on variety.
  • Consume 1 unit: Heal to half of your max health.
  • Consume nothing: Heal to half of your max health, +1 exhaustion.

Destressing

Party members may roleplay with skill checks like performance, cooking etc. to further reduce stress.

  • Each destressing attempt takes 1 time-unit and each party member can only make 1 attempt per long rest.
  • An attempt involves making a DC15 check, which will result in -1 stress to all party members on success.
  • Each success adds +5 to the next check.

Crafting

You are given 4 time-units during long rests.
With the appropriate tools, you can follow the given recipes to make provisions, craft throwables, medicine, potions etc.

Housekeeping

After long resting, remember to go through your inventory and update the timers on your perishable goods.

Foraging/Fishing/Hunting

You can use time-units during rests to forage, if the environment allows. Foraging requires you to specify what you are looking for:

Food

Spend 4 time-units, and gain 1d4 + (foraging/fishing skill) pieces of food.

  • The food you find will depend on the environment and the skill used.
  • In difficult environments, you may need to roll a d20 check as well.
  • Failing the check will result in a 1d4 penalty to the amount of things found.

Medical Herbs/Drugs

Spend 4 time-units, and gain 1 + (foraging skill) pieces of herbs/drug items.

  • The things you find will be dependent on the environment.
  • Items found are: medical herbs, albino beetles, blackriver berries and celusian flowers.
  • In difficult environments, you may need to roll a d20 check as well.
  • Failing the check will result in a 1d4 penalty to the amount of things found.

Hunting

Spend time-units to hunt an animal. You must specify the size of the animal you are trying to hunt. The values you see are a general guide for the GM, they may change depending on environment and circumstance.

The size of the animal changes how you hunt, see the Small Game and Large Game sections below.

Small Game

Small animals like rabbits, fowl, rodents etc. are delicate and die easily. Careless use of heavy weapons can cause the meat and skin to be damaged.
Hunting small game takes place in cycles of 1 time-unit and is split into 2 phases: Tracking and Shooting.
Hunting Cycle:

  • Tracking Phase: Roll a tracking check, DC15
    • On success: Proceed to shooting phase.
    • On failure: Give up and return to camp or proceed to next hunting cycle. For all remaining hunting cycles, you gain +2 to your tracking checks. This stacks.
  • Shooting Phase: Perform an attack roll with a ranged weapon you use for hunting, DC15
    • On hit: See below.
    • On miss: Give up or proceed to next hunting cycle. For all remaining hunting cycles, you gain +2 to your tracking checks. This stacks.
    • You can roll a DC10 stealth check to gain advantage on this attack if successful. On failure, the shot will automatically be considered a miss.
  • Small game have 2-4 health.
    • If your damage roll is below or equal to the animal's health, it dies and you get the normal rewards.
    • If your damage roll is above the animal's health, it dies and the body is considered damaged.
    • You may chose to not include any stat modifiers or other bonuses in your damage roll.
  • On successful hunt: Gain 1d4 raw meat and 1d4 medium grade leather.
    • If the animal's body is damaged: Subtract 1d4 from the meat gains and receive low grade leather instead.
    • If your damage roll matches the animal's health perfectly: Add 1d4 to the meat gains and receive high grade leather instead.
Big Game

Big animals like boar, deer etc. are sturdy and may survive a single shot. It's not uncommon to have to chase down a struck animal multiple times to secure the kill. Taking too many shots to kill the animal can damage the meat and leather.
Hunting large game takes place in cycles of 1 time-unit and is split into 2 phases: Tracking and Shooting.
Hunting Cycle:

  • Tracking Phase: Roll a tracking check, DC12
    • On success: Proceed to shooting phase.
    • On failure: Give up and return to camp or proceed to next hunting cycle. For all remaining hunting cycles, you gain +2 to your tracking checks. This stacks.
  • Shooting Phase: Perform an attack roll with a ranged weapon you use for hunting, DC10
    • On hit: See below.
    • On miss: Give up or proceed to next hunting cycle. For all remaining hunting cycles, you gain +2 to your tracking checks. This stacks.
    • You can roll a DC10 stealth check to gain advantage on this attack if successful. On failure, the shot will automatically be considered a miss.
  • Large game have 10-20 health.
    • If you did not kill the animal but wounded it, you can choose to give up or proceed to the next hunting cycle. For all remaining hunting cycles, you gain +4 to your tracking checks. This stacks.
    • The animal's health is tracked across cycles for that long rest, unless you choose to hunt something else.
  • On successful hunt: Gain 3d4 raw meat and 3d4 medium grade leather.
    • If the animal took 3 or more shots to down: Subtract 1d4 from the meat gains and receive low grade leather instead.
    • If you succeed on the first shot: Add 1d4 to the meat gains and receive high grade leather instead.
    • You cannot initiate another big game hunt if successful for the remainder of the rest.
