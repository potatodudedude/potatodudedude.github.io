Character Creation

Level 1 Character Creation

Welcome dear traveller, to the continent of the laughing titan. This page is a step-by-step guide to setting up your character.

Step 0: Choose your race.

There are 5 distinct types of playable people:

  • Humans from the Aquatine Empire
  • Humans from the Kingdom of Numeria
  • Humans from the Wingard Collective
  • Crocodilians
  • Faeries in Exile

For the sake of party diversity, duplicate races are not allowed, so do consult with everyone else before you sink hours into creating your character!

Go into the individual race page of your chosen race, and take note of the base stats in your character sheet. You can visit the Character Sheet & Stats page to get a comprehensive view of what every stat means.

You may also want to look at the different paths available to each race:

  • Human Paths (Aquatine)
  • Human Paths (Numerian)
  • Human Paths (Wingardian)
  • Crocodilian Paths
  • Faery Paths
    Paths are lifestyles your character can take on later down the line that have gameplay effects, so being familiar with them now can help in your backstory creation.

Step 1: Outline your backstory.

You don't have to write a 12 page essay on your character, but there are a few questions that you should answer when creating your character. You may look at the different paths each race has for inspiration. The answers to these questions can be tightly interconnected or distinct and separate.

Why do you want to venture?

More specifically, what is your character's motivation to take risks and go along dangerous missions instead of just playing it safe and surviving? The aim here is to provide your character with some end-goal they are working towards. Your reason can be something like:

  • I want to make a difference in this bleak world.
  • I'm tired of being at the bottom, and I have lofty ambitions of being in power.
  • I want to become stronger/more influential to right a wrong I committed/was done against me.
  • I don't want to starve to death like the rest of my friends.

Why do you want to venture with a party?

This is essential to make sure your character has a vested interest in keeping the party together and working as a team. Your reason can be:

  • I really like talking to people.
  • I'm afraid of sleeping alone at night.
  • I'm not confident in myself and my abilities.
  • I crave companionship to replace the family I lost.

What is someplace/something/someone that is significant to you?

This is so I have something to put into the game tailored for you! What is something very important to your character that is not currently with them? This can be a place, a thing, a person, a pet. It can be good or bad. Of course, you will likely need to provide me with specific details for me to integrate it, so don't feel shy to reach out to me to brainstorm ideas during the campaign! Your ideas can be:

  • My late mother's blueberry pie recipe, now lost.
  • A favourite bakery when I was young.
  • A lost family member/friend.
  • A vendetta against a murderer.

What have you lost due to the invasion?

The campaign's themes revolve heavily around loss and the messy process of recovery. Imagine the life your character would have lived if the Nebulans never made contact. What you've lost can be literal, like family, friends, property etc. It can be abstract, like a potential childhood snatched away, or the loss of your national identity or faith. You may even choose to have your character suffer no real personal loss as a result of the invasion, and have them serve as a juxtaposition to the rest of the world. Likewise, how much this loss affects your character and whether you want them to work on getting over it is up to you.

Step 2: Allocate your core stats.

Now, it's time to allocate points to your stats. Core stat allocations are bound by these rules:

  • All core stats begin at 0. Race-specific bonuses/maluses are applied after point allocation.
  • You get 2 points for core physical stats and 2 points for core mental stats.
  • Point costs become more expensive the higher the stat is:
    • -1 to 0: 1
    • 0 to 1: 1
    • 1 to 2: 2
  • You can lower a stat below 0 to gain a point.
  • Stats cannot go lower than -1, and cannot go higher than +2 during character creation(before racial bonuses/maluses).

Make sure to update your initiative, equipment load thresholds and carrying capacity based on your new stats and your race.

Step 3: Allocate Skills

Pick 2 major skills and 3 minor skills. These skills will start at level 1. Each race has racial-specific skills, you add to those skills after picking the 5 skills. Skill level cannot go above 3.

Aquatine humans gain +1 major/minor skill, or add 1 to the level of an existing major/minor skill.

Step 4: Allocate Weapon Proficiencies

Now, you get to choose 1 tier 1 weapon proficiency for free. Look at the melee and ranged weapon folders to see your options! Note that certain weapons that need special training from certain factions cannot be chosen.

Bear in mind your race comes with its own complementary proficiencies, some weapons have restrictions to learning their proficiencies, and some weapons already grant tier 1 proficiency for free. See Weapon Rules for more information.

Step 5: Decide Starting Gear.

Common Gear:

All races begin with this:

  • 2 bandages.
    In addition, you can choose 1 weapon.
  • The weapon must cost less than 300g.
  • If you chose a non-thrown ranged weapon, you will get 10 pieces of the relevant basic ammunition.
  • If you chose a thrown ranged weapon, you will get 3 pieces of the weapon.
    You may choose to exchange the starting equipment for gold or other items of equivalent value.

Humans (Aquatine):

Aquatine humans start with:

  • 1 Sharp Stick.
  • 1 Militia's Shield.
  • 1 Commoner's Aketon.

Humans (Numerian)

Numerian humans start with:

  • 1 Smith's Hammer.
  • 1 Commoner's Aketon.

Humans (Wingardian)

Wingardian humans start with:

  • 1 Great Knife.
  • 1 Repurposed Dinner Bowl
  • 1 Commoner's Aketon.
  • 50g

Crocodilians

Crocodilians start with:

  • 1 Woodcutter.

Faeries

Faeries start with:

  • 1 Crude Flat Blade or 1 Sharp Stick.
  • 1 Arming Jacket

Step 6: Draw Appearance

Now all the technical stuff is done, draw your character out, either digitally or on paper and send it with your character sheet. The drawing need not be pretty or nice, you can draw simple stick figures if you want. What's important is to include distinctive features or characteristics your character has, so that the GM and other players can get an idea of what you're visualising as your character!

Congratulations! You are now ready to start playing! Unless you're playing a faery, in which case continue on to create your pixie ;P.

Pixie Creation

If you're playing as a faery, you will also have to create a pixie.

Core Stat Allocation

Pixies are not playable characters, and do not engage in combat the same way as the playable races. As such, only their core mental stats are alterable:

  • Intelligence cannot be changed.
  • The remaining core mental stats start at 0.
  • You get 2 points.
  • Point costs become more expensive the higher the stat is:
    • -1 to 0: 1
    • 0 to 1: 1
    • 1 to 2: 2
  • You can lower a stat below 0 to gain a point.
  • Stats cannot go lower than -1, and cannot go higher than +2 during character creation.
  • These are largely just for show and character consistency, as your faery is still the one that will be doing most ability checks.
