Damage Calculations

To determine the effect of an attack on someone, there are two layers of defense.

Melee/Ranged Defence:

Melee/Ranged defence is how you avoid getting hit at all from the respective attacks. This is determined by the AC system from DnD. You make an attack roll with relevant modifiers, and see if it's higher than the opponent's relevant defence score. If so, you hit. Otherwise, they dodge or parry or whatever, and you do miss.

Bear in mind you need to roll higher, not not higher than or equal to like in DnD 5e. Rolling equal to the enemy's defence will give the choice of succeeding the attack with complications or failing.

By default, all races have a base melee and ranged defence of 10, apart from crocodilians who only have 8 ranged defence.

Regular Damage:

This simply refers to normal damage that is affected by the armour and penetration mechanics detailed below. It is distinct from Blunt Force Trauma and Direct Damage. If not otherwise stated, whenever you see "damage" in this wiki, it is referring to regular damage.

Direct Damage:

Direct damage is distinct from regular damage, it is not affected by armour and penetration mechanics. Direct damage is typically used for status effects like bleeding and burning. Depending on the source, direct damage may be subject to certain effects. For example, direct damage from burning is reduced by the Lightbearer path, and direct damage from Neurotoxin bypasses temporary health.

Armour:

The regular damage of an attack is negated by armour. Simply subtract the armour value from the regular damage.

Penetration:

Some weapons/moves have penetration values. Penetration reduces the armour value by that amount during damage calculations.

Effective Armour:

This is how much armour you have left after accounting for the attacker's penetration stat. Essentially it's just (your armour) - (enemy's penetration). Effective armour cannot drop below 0.

Blunt Force Trauma:

Some weapons/moves deal blunt force. This damage ignores armour and serves as additional damage on top of your regular damage.

Rounding:

By default, all values are rounded up.

Calculation Example:

For example, if you use a melee attack with these values:

Strength: 2
Dexterity: 2
Hit chance: +Dexterity
Damage: 1d6 + 2
Penetration: +2
Blunt Force Trauma: +Strength

Against an enemy with these defences:

Armour: 4
Melee defence: 12

Hit chance:

You would first roll a d20 and add your hit chance modifier. If this resulting value is higher than the enemy's melee defence, you hit.

Let's say we rolled 11, we add our hit chance bonus modifier, which is +Dexterity. We have 2 Dexterity, so we add 2.

Hit chance = 11 from d20 roll + 2 from modifier bonus = 13

Since our hit chance of 13 is just greater than their melee defence of 12, we just barely hit!

Damage:

We must now roll for damage. Our regular damage is 1d6 + 2, meaning we roll a d6, then add the +2 modifier to the value.

Let's say we roll a 4 on the d6, now the calculations are as follows:

Add damage modifiers:
4 rolled damage + 2 modifier bonus damage = 6 regular damage before armour

Calculate effective armour by subtracting penetration from it:
4 enemy armour - 2 penetration = 2 effective enemy armour

Calculate how much regular damage we do by subtracting the enemy's effective armour:
6 regular damage - 2 effective enemy armour = 4 regular damage to enemy

Finally, add on blunt damage, which in this case is our Strength:
4 regular damage + 2 blunt force trauma = 6 total damage to enemy's health.

Quick hacks for getting a sense of how your damage output is:

The enemy's effective melee defence divided by 20 gives you the odds of hitting. Effective melee defence = melee defence - hit chance.

E.g. An enemy with 10 effective melee defence gives you 50% odds to hit them.

To calculate the average value of a die, sum its minimum and maximum and divide by 2.

E.g. The average of a d6 = (1 + 6) / 2 = 3.5
