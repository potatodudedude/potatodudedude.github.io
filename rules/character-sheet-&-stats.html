Character Sheet & Stats

Health:

Self-explanatory. When health reaches 0, you enter a moment of mortality.

Temporary Health:

Additional health on top of your health bar that gets depleted first. Temporary health cannot be restored, and is lost on long rest. Temporary health is considered as regular health for all calculations, such as for reaper's grace.

Gaining more temporary health when you already have some will override your existing value if the new value is greater. If it isn't, then nothing happens.

Armour:

Represents protection from damage. Armour is mostly gained through equipment. In some popular ttrpgs, such as DND, armour is used to determine if an attack roll will hit, but here that role is fulfilled by Melee Defence and Ranged Defence.

Instead, armour reduces damage once you are hit: Your armour value is subtracted from the incoming damage. More detailed calculation details and how armour interacts with penetration and blunt force is discussed in Damage Calculations.

Temporary Armour:

Some customised armour pieces grant temporary armour. Temporary armour acts as a bonus to your regular armour, but degrades with every hit you take. It can be restored by repairing your armour piece during rests.

Stress

Shamelessly ripped off from darkest dungeon. When stress reaches max, it reduces to 0 and you suffer a temporary insanity until 2 long rests have passed, or a duration at the GM's discretion.

Stress accumulates from reaching critical health, having allies die, failing critically and generally whenever something bad happens.

Stress is reduced when resting, winning tough fights, succeeding critically and generally whenever something good happens.

Veterancy

Veterancy is a stat mainly used to cap certain abilities as a balancing method. It is calculated by your level/3, rounded up.
Levels 0 - 3: Veterancy 1
Levels 4 - 6: Veterancy 2
Levels 7 - 9: Veterancy 3

Core Stats:

Core stats start from an average of 0, and are added directly as modifiers to skill checks. Due to the lack of spellcasting, mental stats are not as prevalent, hence during character creation, your stat point pool is split into two: physical and mental.

Physical Stats:

Strength

Combat: Important for strength based weapons like axes and certain bows. Usually used to determine additional blunt force trauma damage, useful against armour. Also grants additional health and equipment load thresholds, but not as much as endurance.
Roleplay: Used for activities requiring burst power. (e.g. lifting, throwing, smashing)

Dexterity

Combat: Important for dexterity based weapons like swords. Usually used to determine additional defences, helping survivability.
Roleplay: Used for activities requiring speed and agility. (e.g. running, acrobatics, pickpocketing)

Endurance

Combat: Main stat for increasing health and equipment load thresholds, as well as resisting negative effects like poison.
Roleplay: Used for activities requiring sustained power or resisting effects. (e.g. negating poison, carrying heavy loads for a long time)

Mental Stats:

Intelligence

Roleplay: Used for technical and scholarly knowledge. (e.g. recalling history, brewing chemicals, performing first aid)

Wisdom

Roleplay: Used for intuition and observation. (e.g. scouting, figuring out lies, finding patterns)

Charisma

Roleplay: Used for social interactions. (e.g. persuading, lying, bartering)

Physical Capabilities:

Movement Speed:

How far you can move in a single round of combat. Default is 4m, with Faeries having 5m.

Jump Distance

How far you can jump. Default is 2m, with Faeries having 3m.

Initiative

Initiative determines who goes first during the zig-zag cycle of combat.
By default, initiative is calculated by a d20 + dexterity, but Faeries have an additional +2 initiative, and being in light load allows you to double your dexterity modifier.

Melee Defence

The value the enemy's melee attack roll needs to be higher than to hit. All races have a default value of 10, and add their veterancy.

Ranged Defence

The value the enemy's ranged attack roll needs to be higher than to hit. Most races have a default value of 10 (Crocodilians have 8), and add their veterancy.

Equipment

Think of your inventory as split into two different groups:

  • Equipment Load, which represents weapons, armour and items that are readily accessible and that you can use in combat.
  • Backpack Load, which represents items that you won't use in combat, such as treasure, food, tools etc.

Equipment Load:

How much combat ready equipment you can equip. Any items you want to use in combat or secondary weapons you want to swap to count towards your equipment load.
If an item is not in your equipment load, you cannot readily use it. This includes medicine and armour.

Each point in endurance increases the thresholds by 2kg. Each point of negative endurance subtracts 2kg instead.
Each point of strength increases the thresholds by 1kg. Each point of negative strength subtracts 1kg instead.

The default thresholds are:

  • Light load (<10kg): +1m movement speed and jump distance, and...
    • Add your dexterity score as melee defence (capped by your Veterancy).
    • Add your dexterity score as ranged defence (capped by your Veterancy).
    • Add your dexterity score as initiative.
  • Medium load (>=10, <20kg):
    • You get no penalties or benefits.
  • High load (>=20, <30kg):
    • -1m movement speed.
    • -1m jump distance.
    • -2 initiative.
    • Take +1 blunt force trauma every level when taking fall damage.
  • Overloaded (>=30, <40kg):
    • -2m movement speed.
    • -2m jump distance.
    • -4 initiative
    • -1 to strength and dexterity.
    • Take +2 blunt force trauma every level when taking fall damage.
  • Rock Cosplay(>=40kg):
    • Your movement speed is capped to 1m
    • You cannot jump.
    • You always go last in initiative.
    • Strength and Dexterity are set to -4.
    • Take +3 blunt force trauma every level when taking fall damage.
      Crocodilians have different thresholds.

Backpack Load

Backpack load represents all the stuff you are carrying that is not in your equipment load. You cannot access your backpack items readily during combat.

Your characters will usually jettison their backpacks at the start of combat. Choosing to fight with your backpack on will result in your backpack weight being added to your equipment load.

Carrying Capacity:

How much you can carry in total, your equipment load + backpack load added together. Default is 70kg, with each point of endurance adding 5kg. Each point of negative endurance subtracts 5kg instead.

Carrying capacity cannot go below the maximum threshold of your equipment load.

Going overweight will result in disadvantage on all rolls.

Proficiencies

Weapon proficiencies determine which weapons you can wield properly. Higher proficiencies unlocks more weapon moves and can also improve stats.

Traits

Traits are perks with specific functions. You get them every level up.

Paths

Paths are lifestyles your character chooses that influence their gameplay. You get to choose a path at level 3, which then levels up every subsequent 3rd level. See the path folder under playable races for more details.

Skills:

Skills are a representation of what your character specialises in beyond their generic Core Stats. They are split into 2 types:

  • Major: Major skills are those that are more likely to be used/useful, or have some crafting system tied to them.
  • Minor: Minor skills are niche skills that are less likely to be used and have no systems tied to them.

When do these skills come into play? They are used in skill checks, where you make a d20 roll with your skill and core stat modifiers against a difficulty class specified by the GM. For instance:

  • GM asks: "Make a scouting check with dexterity, DC15". This means to roll a d20 with your dexterity and scouting skill added as modifiers against a target of 15.
  • You ask: "Can I use my acrobatics with dexterity to avoid the trap?". This means you want to make a d20 check with your dexterity and acrobatics skill added as modifiers.
  • When doing these skill checks, you are restricted to only using 1 skill and 1 core stat at a time.
    • The exception is high/low class mannerisms, which can be added to another skill if it makes sense.
  • If you lack the appropriate skill to achieve something, you may be asked to roll with disadvantage, roll against a higher DC or not be allowed to roll at all.

Skills start at 1 and go up to a maximum of 3. Note that 1 is not the "minimum", not having the skill at all is the "minimum".

Major Skills

  • Acrobatics
    • Used to perform acts of agility, like jumping from rooftops, dodging traps etc.
  • Alchemy
    • Allows you to do Alchemy, creating your own customised potions and medicine.
  • Armour-Smithing
    • Allows you to do Armour Smithing, creating your own customised armour pieces.
  • Bartering
    • Used in haggling, getting better trade deals etc.
  • Cooking
    • Used to cook better meals that grant bonuses to the party.
  • Deception
    • Used to lie.
  • Fishing
    • Used to obtain food in the appropriate environment.
  • Foraging
    • Used to obtain food/herbs in the appropriate environment.
  • Gunpowder Chemistry
    • Allows you to do Gunpowder Chemistry, creating your own customised gunpowder consumables.
  • Hunting
    • Used to obtain food by hunting animals.
  • Intimidation
    • Used to align people to your views and perspectives in an aggressive and not-nice manner.
  • Medicine
    • Used in healing and curing ailments.
  • Night Vision
    • Allows you to see further in dark conditions, see lighting rules.
  • Persuasion
    • Used to align people to your views and perspectives in a calm and nice manner.
  • Riding
    • Used in mounted combat and training horses faster.
  • Sailing
    • Used when acting as captain of a ship.
  • Scouting
    • Used for detecting oddities, finding clues or enemies.
  • Stealth
    • Used for sneaking around, hiding things.
  • Swimming
    • Used for movement in water.
  • Weapon-Smithing
    • Allows you to do Weapon Smithing, creating your own customised weapons and ammunition.
