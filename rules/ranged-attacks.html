Ranged Attacks

To make ranged gameplay more interesting than just siting in the back shooting all day, I decided to introduce some new mechanics to make ranged gameplay more interesting.

Move sets:

Each weapon type now has a unique set of moves. Moves can be unlocked by investing proficiency points during level ups.

Range:

Each ranged weapon now has three values tied to range: Optimal Range, Range Penalty and Maximum Range.

Optimal Range:

Imagine a weapon's total range is cut up into intervals, which each interval being the same length as its optimal range. The weapon incurs one instance of range penalty for every interval of the optimal range.

Range Penalty:

For every interval of the optimal range, suffer some defined penalty. There is a maximum amount of range penalties that can be applied.
Damage cannot be reduced below 1, penetration and blunt force trauma cannot be reduced below 0.

Maximum Range:

Weapons cannot shoot past this.

For instance, let's say a weapon has an optimal range of 5m, and a range penalty of -1 hit chance that can be applied 5 times.
At 6m, the weapon would incur -1 hit chance. For every additional 5m after, it would incur an additional -1 hit chance. Once the maximum -5 hit chance penalty has been reached at 26m, no further penalties will be incurred.

View Cones and Flanking Fire:

Ranged attacks behave the same as melee attacks when it comes to view cone restrictions and flanking fire, with the exception that view cone stretching does not apply to ranged attacks.

Line of Sight:

Most ranged weapons have relatively flat trajectories, meaning line-of-sight is now a mechanic. No more shooting through the back of your fighter into the enemy.

Obstructions:

First, see if you can even see the target. If you cannot draw an unobstructed line from any corner of your tile to any corner of the target's, you cannot see them. If you can see the target, draw a line from any corner of your character's tile to the center of the target's. If the line is obstructed, roll with disadvantage for hit chance:

  • If both rolls succeed: You hit anyway.
  • If one roll succeeds: You hit the obstruction instead.
  • If both rolls fail: You don't hit.
    If there is more than one obstruction, they are considered fully obstructed and you cannot hit the target at all.

Height advantage/disadvantages:

If your target is at a lower elevation compared to you, roll with advantage for hit chance.
If your target is at a higher elevation compared to you, roll with disadvantage for hit chance.

Advantage/Disadvantage can cancel each other out (e.g. shooting from high ground at an obstructed target will result in a normal hit chance roll)

Advantage/Disadvantages do not stack (e.g. shooting from low ground at an obstructed target will result in a regular disadvantaged roll)

Threatened

Ranged attacks suffer disadvantage to hit chance if you're within or closer than the default optimal range of an enemy's melee weapon.
This does not apply if you're flanking the enemy.
